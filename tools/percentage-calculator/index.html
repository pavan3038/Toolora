      About Percentage Calculator

      A percentage is a number expressed as a fraction of 100. “20%” means 20 out of every 100, or 0.20. Percentages make it easy to compare amounts of different sizes, which is why they appear in discounts, tax, interest, exam marks and statistics.

      The formulas used here are: X% of Y = Y × X ÷ 100. To find what percent X is of Y, divide X by Y and multiply by 100. Percentage change from X to Y = (Y − X) ÷ |X| × 100, where a positive result is an increase and a negative result is a decrease. To increase X by Y%, add X × Y ÷ 100 to X; to decrease it, subtract that amount. To find the whole when you know a part, divide the part by the percentage and multiply by 100.

      A few worked examples: 20% of 150 is 30. A price that rises from 80 to 100 has increased by 25%. A 15% discount on 1,500 saves 225, leaving 1,275. If 30 is 20% of a number, that number is 150.

      Remember that percentage change is not symmetrical: a price that goes up 50% and then down 50% does not return to where it started, because the second percentage is taken from a larger number. Also note that a change from 0 cannot be expressed as a percentage, because dividing by zero is undefined.

      Percentage Calculator – frequently asked questions

      How do I calculate a percentage of a number?

      Multiply the number by the percentage and divide by 100. For example, 20% of 150 is 150 × 20 ÷ 100 = 30. The first calculator on this page does it for you.

      How do I calculate percentage increase or decrease?

      Subtract the old value from the new value, divide by the old value and multiply by 100. A positive answer is an increase and a negative answer is a decrease. Use the “percentage change” calculator for this.

      How do I work out a discount?

      Use “Decrease X by Y%”: enter the original price as X and the discount percentage as Y. The result is the price after the discount. To see how much you save, use “What is X% of Y?” with the same numbers.

      What is the difference between percent and percentage points?

      If an interest rate rises from 4% to 5%, it has gone up by 1 percentage point, but by 25 percent in relative terms. This calculator’s “percentage change” gives the relative figure.

      Why does it say it cannot calculate a change from zero?

      A percentage change divides by the starting value, and dividing by zero is undefined. If you start from zero, any increase is infinite in percentage terms, so there is no meaningful percentage.

      Can I use negative numbers or decimals?

      Yes. You can enter negative numbers and decimals using a dot, and thousands separators such as 1,500 are accepted.
