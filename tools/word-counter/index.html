      How to use Word Counter

      1. Type or paste your text into the box — or use Upload to open a .txt file.
      2. Read your counts, estimated reading and speaking time, and the most repeated words.
      3. Check the limit meters if you are writing for X, a meta description or an SEO title. Use Copy, Download or Clear when you are done.

      About Word Counter

      Word limits are everywhere: school essays, job applications, article briefs, product descriptions and social posts. This counter gives you the number that matters straight away, along with characters (with and without spaces), sentences and paragraphs.

      Words are counted the way word processors count them: any group of letters or digits separated by spaces or line breaks is one word, so “well-known” is one word and a lone “–” or “&” is not counted. Languages that do not use spaces between words, such as Chinese, Japanese and Thai, are split into words using your browser’s built-in language rules. Characters are counted as people see them, so an emoji or a Hindi conjunct such as “क्ष” counts as one character.

      Reading time assumes an average adult reads about 200 words per minute and speaking time assumes about 130 words per minute, a comfortable pace for a speech or voice-over. These are estimates — difficult material or a slower delivery will take longer.

      The keyword list shows the words you use most often, which is a quick way to spot overused words in an essay or to check the main topic of an article. By default it ignores common English words such as “the” and “and”. The limit meters show your progress against typical limits: a post on X (280, approximate because links and some characters are weighted differently), a search-result meta description (about 160) and an SEO page title (about 60).

      How your data is handled

      Your text is analysed by your browser as you type. It is not uploaded, saved or logged — when you close the page, it is gone.

      Word Counter – frequently asked questions

      How does the word counter count words?

      Any run of letters or digits separated by spaces or line breaks counts as one word, like in Microsoft Word and Google Docs. Hyphenated words such as “well-known” count as one, and standalone symbols are ignored.

      Is my text saved or uploaded?

      No. The text stays in your browser and is not stored or sent anywhere. Closing the tab clears it.

      Does it work for Hindi, Chinese or other languages?

      Yes. Hindi and other space-separated languages are counted by words and characters as people see them. Chinese, Japanese and Thai are split into words using your browser’s language data.

      How is reading time calculated?

      By dividing the word count by 200 words per minute for silent reading, and by 130 words per minute for speaking aloud. Real speeds vary by person and content.

      What is the difference between characters and characters without spaces?

      The first counts everything, including spaces and line breaks. The second counts only visible characters. Different platforms use different rules, so check which one your limit refers to.

      Why do my character counts differ from another tool?

      Tools disagree on how they count line breaks, emoji and combining characters. Toolora counts what you see: one emoji is one character, and a line break counts as one.
