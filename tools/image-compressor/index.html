        How to use Image Compressor

        1. Drop your images onto the box, click it to choose files, or paste an image from your clipboard.
        2. Pick a quality level. 70–80 is a good balance for photos. Optionally choose WebP and a maximum size.
        3. Check the before-and-after sizes, then download each image or all of them as one ZIP file.

        About Image Compressor

        Large images slow down websites, fill up inboxes and fail upload forms with “file too large” errors. Compressing an image re-encodes it with less detail than the original — detail that is usually invisible at normal viewing size — so the file becomes much smaller.

        JPEG and WebP are “lossy” formats: the quality slider trades file size for fine detail. For most photos a setting between 70 and 80 is visually indistinguishable from the original while saving half the file size or more. PNG is a “lossless” format, so re-encoding a PNG in a browser saves very little. If you need a much smaller file from a PNG photo, choose WebP or JPEG as the output format. A transparent PNG converted to WebP keeps its transparency; converted to JPEG, transparent areas become white.

        Reducing the pixel dimensions usually saves more space than lowering quality. A 4000-pixel-wide photo is far bigger than most screens need, so setting a maximum size such as 1920 pixels is often the single most effective step. If the result would be larger than your original, Toolora keeps your original file instead and tells you it is already well optimised.

        Because compression re-draws each image, metadata stored inside the file — such as camera model and GPS location — is removed from the compressed copy. Photos taken in portrait orientation are rotated correctly before saving, so they do not turn sideways.

        How your data is handled

        Your images are decoded and compressed by your browser on your own device. They are not uploaded to any server and nothing is stored after you close the page.

        Image Compressor – frequently asked questions

        Are my images uploaded to a server?

        No. Compression runs entirely in your browser using your device’s own image engine. You can confirm this by opening your browser’s network tab while compressing — no image data is sent anywhere.

        What quality setting should I use?

        For photos, 70–80 is a good starting point: file sizes drop sharply with very little visible difference. Use 85–90 for images with fine text or gradients, and go lower (50–60) when file size matters more than perfection, such as for small thumbnails.

        Why did my PNG barely shrink?

        PNG is lossless, so there is little to remove. To make a PNG photo much smaller, choose WebP or JPEG as the output format, or reduce its maximum size. Screenshots and graphics with flat colours compress well as WebP.

        Does it keep transparency?

        Yes for PNG and WebP output. JPEG does not support transparency, so transparent areas are filled with white when you convert to JPEG.

        Is there a limit on the number or size of images?

        You can add up to 20 images at a time, each up to 25 MB. Very large photos need a lot of memory to process, so on older phones you may get best results with a few images at a time.

        Why can’t I choose WebP in my browser?

        Some browsers, including Safari, cannot create WebP files, so Toolora disables the option there. JPEG works everywhere.

        Frequently Asked Questions

        Will compressing reduce photo clarity?

        No. The compression algorithm optimizes color data and strips redundant metadata while maintaining visual sharpness.

        Can I compress pictures to 100KB or 50KB?

        Yes. Simply adjust the quality slider down until the live preview shows your required KB size.

        Are my images stored on any server?

        No. All compression processing happens entirely inside your web browser. Your files never leave your device.
